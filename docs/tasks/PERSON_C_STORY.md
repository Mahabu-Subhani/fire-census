# Person C: story, validation of claims, and submission

You own `docs/` and `submission/`. Most of the judging is based on the project
page, and Presentation is one of five equally weighted scores. Your work decides
whether judges understand what the other two built.

Read first: `AGENTS.md`, `docs/CONTEXT.md` (judging criteria, rules, page
fields), `docs/APPROACH.md` (so you can explain the method in plain words).

## Before the start (allowed prep only)

- Ask the Local Lead for the exact local submission and judging times, and
  whether there is a live pitch. Record the answer in `docs/CONTEXT.md`.
- Watch the demos of past Global Winners on spaceappschallenge.org and note what
  their first five seconds do.
- Decide with the team: video or slides. A video must be 30 seconds or less.
- Do not write project page text or record anything yet.

## Phase 1: Sat 10:15 to 13:00. Structure

1. Open the Project tab on the team page and save a first draft of every field,
   even if rough. Use the edit lock so nobody overwrites you.
2. Create `docs/REFERENCES.md` and `docs/AI_USE.md` logs and ask A and B to add
   to them as they work.
3. Write the one-sentence pitch and get both teammates to agree on it.
4. Storyboard the demo against the official timing: 0:00 names, 0:02 the
   challenge, 0:05 project name and tagline, 0:10 how it works with NASA data,
   0:20 to 0:30 the future.
5. Find one real, citable example of a fire manager's need in each demo region.

**Checkpoint 13:00:** draft saved in every project page field; storyboard agreed.

## Phase 2: Sat 14:30 to 19:30. Explain the method

1. Write the method in plain language for an emergency manager: two reasons the
   sensors disagree (resolution, sensitivity), one step for each, and what the
   small-fire layer is.
2. Choose and test the accessible colour ramp with Person B.
3. Act as the first user. Use the app every hour and write down every point of
   confusion for Person B.
4. Check every factual claim you plan to make against a source. Remove what you
   cannot support.

**Checkpoint 19:30:** method text drafted; you can explain the approach aloud
in 30 seconds without notes.

## Phase 3: Sat night to Sun 12:30. Evidence and demo

1. Get the test results and figures from Person A. Write one honest sentence
   for each test, including any that failed.
2. Write the detailed description: what it does, how it works, benefits, what
   we hope to achieve, tools used.
3. Fill "NASA data" (FIRMS products, exact names) and "References" from the logs.
4. Fill "Use of AI" from `docs/AI_USE.md`, truthfully.
5. Record the demo from the live app. Lead with the raw record's jump, then
   switch to harmonized. If a human under 18 is on the team, they must not
   appear or be heard.
6. Add a "Limits" section. Judges trust projects that state their limits.

**Checkpoint Sun 12:30:** demo recorded and under the limit; page text complete.

## Phase 4: Sun 13:30 to 16:00. Submit

1. Upload the demo to a public host. Open every link in a private window.
2. Tick both agreement boxes and click **Submit for Judging** by 15:30. You can
   edit and resubmit until 23:59.
3. Confirm the success banner. Screenshot it.
4. Keep polishing wording until the deadline. Do not change numbers without
   Person A.

## Rules for your work

- Every number on the page comes from Person A's `meta.json`. Do not round in a
  way that flatters the result.
- No NASA logos. Any AI-generated image or video carries a visible watermark.
- English only.
